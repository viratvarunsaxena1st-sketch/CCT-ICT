Chimera

A chimera, simply speaking, is a monster made up of parts of different animals. This tool is the combination of many effective training tools and, hopefully, is better than those tools. It is thus aptly named — Chimera.

How this works

Digits from 1–9 are read aloud, one at a time. Add each new digit to the one spoken right before it — not a running total. A number flashes on screen after every pair: decide whether it's that sum.

  • It is the sum → press Left
  • It's the sum, off by one → press Right
  • Anything else → press nothing

Spacebar skip — press Space any time a number is on screen to dismiss it early instead of waiting it out, so you can move on the moment you've made your decision. On sum and ±1 trials this is just a shortcut past the number and never affects scoring.

On a withhold trial, though, Space is now the required response rather than a pure shortcut: press it to mark that trial correct. Let a withhold trial pass without pressing it and it's now scored wrong, instead of the usual rule where doing nothing is correct.

N-back mode — instead of pairing each digit with the one spoken right before it, pair it with the digit from 2 digits back. Everything else works the same: judge the flashed number against that sum.

Works with dichotic attention switching too — the sequence carries across ear switches, so a higher level stays trackable even as the voice changes.

Dichotic attention switching (experimental) — every interval, two different digits now play at once, one in each ear. A short beep in one ear tells you which ear to follow. Add and judge digits from that ear only, and let the other ear pass by unattended — until the beep plays in the other ear.

The two ears also speak in different voices — male in the left ear, female in the right ear — as a constant cue for which stream is which, on top of the beep.

  • A beep in your left ear → follow the left ear from then on.
  • A beep in your right ear → follow the right ear from then on.
  • The target keeps switching every few trials with no warning besides that beep, so listen for it.
  • Your running addition carries across the switch — keep adding onto the same sequence, just from the newly-cued ear.

Needs stereo headphones — the two streams won't separate on a single mono speaker. The beep uses the beep volume setting below.

eWMT mode — while the number is on screen, the square behind it also shows an image, drawn from whichever categories you've selected below. The image appears and disappears in step with the number itself; it has no effect on scoring. Pick Custom to draw from images you upload yourself — they're stored locally in this browser, not uploaded anywhere.
